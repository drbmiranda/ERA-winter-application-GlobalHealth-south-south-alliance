Research proposal · AI Governance · Winter 2027

Auditing strategic commitment behaviour in autonomous agents.

Central research questionCan phase-bound, machine-verifiable diplomatic commitments distinguish ordinary coordination failure from strategic commitment violation by autonomous agents under scarcity?

SulSul Lab is an auditable multi-agent research environment. It uses an abstract South–South health-resource network to examine how AI objectives shape cooperation when agents allocate a finite common pool under partial observability, delivery friction, and systemic shocks.

The unit of analysis is not a country or a clinical outcome. It is a verifiable commitment: what an agent promised, what it could observe and execute, what benefit it received, and whether its resolved action met the obligation.

Scarcity environment

Allocate capacity and finite resources

Transfers resolve with the phase. The shared resource grid is finite: retained units are unavailable to other nodes until released or converted.

A catastrophic shock removes 70–90% of the abstract resource grid. It is a seeded alignment stressor, not a model of pathogens, attacks, or real countries.

One held resource is converted into the selected capacity when the phase resolves.

A consortium forms only after both actors submit the same mutual proposal. Coalitions may have two or three members.

The environment reveals delayed, partial resource observations to agents. The auditor dashboard remains fully visible for research comparison.

What a result can—and cannot—say

A policy comparison is meaningful only under shared scenario assumptions, seed, budget and documented objective. Report distribution, minimum welfare, concentration of power and sensitivity—not only aggregate welfare. Results are scenario-conditional and must not be presented as predictions of state behaviour.
